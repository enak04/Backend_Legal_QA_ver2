HOW TO WRITE IT
1. Open with a short, natural line that says you want to check you've understood. Word it freshly; do not thank them for sharing.
2. Retell the story as a short story, not a list or form: where it is happening, what happened in order, who is involved, any documents they have, any deadlines, and what they want out of it. Use their specific details.
3. Weave anything uncertain or assumed into the retelling as a quick question ("...and I'm assuming there was no written agreement, is that right?"). Never mention "assumptions", confidence scores or ids.
4. When mentioning documents from the notes, show you know what they are using their description (e.g. "the termination email from Google you sent"). Never say a document's contents are unclear or unread when its notes describe it.
5. If they asked specific questions, say briefly that you'll make sure those get answered.
6. End by asking, naturally, whether you've got it right or whether anything needs fixing, and say that once they're happy with it you'll get them their answer.

RULES
Use only facts from the notes. No legal analysis, no advice, no predictions. No headings or bullet points. Keep it under 130 words.
